## Decisions

- Kept the planner's three tasks as drafted, one per layer. T-1991 waits for none and builds the generator, the benchmarks, and the reference replay. T-1993 waits for T-1991 because it proves the fix against that reference and benchmark. T-1995 waits for T-1993 because it measures the fixed replay on this repository and closes I-0155. No two tasks run together: each needs the one before.
- T-1991's profile, over a generated history of 500 stories and 1,931 tasks tuned to this repository's density (about 2 stories ready, 1 in progress, 1 in review at a time): `replayHolds` takes 2.7 s, 1.07 GB, and 14.3 M allocations per replay, 96 percent of `Compute`. Of 9,779 distinct event times, 8,326 build a `Holds`, and only 4,240 change anything a hold reads. Cumulative shares: `replayHolds` 73 percent, `NewHolds` 38, `Holds.Of` 27, `PathsOverlap` 21 (its `b+"/"` concatenations), `Holds.Overlaps` 18, `Holds.Claim` 13, the garbage collector 17.
- The fix T-1993 builds, chosen from that profile: judge the holds again only at an event time where a story event happened, or a task of a story in ready or in progress was created or moved, and otherwise carry the previous instant's held stories with their codes. Build each `Holds` from only the stories in ready or in progress, the stories their `after` names, and their tasks, kept up to date as the events apply, rather than over every item created so far. Make `PathsOverlap` compare without concatenating, which the board's holds gain from too. Per-story claim reuse is left out: with about three stories open at a time it saves little once the `Holds` is small.
- That first pass of T-1993 took a replay from 2,726 ms to 249–282 ms, with results identical to the reference, and judged the holds at 3,693 event times. A story's move counts only into or out of ready, in progress, or done, and a task's only into or out of done or cancelled, since those are the only states a hold reads. The profile then put 29 percent in `Holds.Claim` and 18 percent in `manifest.Claims.Covers`, so the earlier decision to leave out claim reuse was reversed. `workitem.HoldsMemo` remembers each story's claim, which shared paths cover, and how one claim holds another, across the `Holds` built from `memo.NewHolds`. The replay forgets a story's claim when one of its tasks is created or enters or leaves done or cancelled. Holds built with `NewHolds` carry no memo and judge as before. The board, the pull hold, `flai check`, and `flai serve` all use them. One replay now takes 71–72 ms, 26.8 MB, and 275 k allocations, against 2,726 ms, 1.07 GB, and 14.3 M. `BenchmarkComputeClaims` takes 134–147 ms. `holdBy` now stops at the first holding pair, as its reason already did, rather than building every pair.
- T-1995 measured both builds on this repository on 2026-10-21, between 12:41 and 12:47 UTC, under a load average of 11 to 21. `stats.get` warm went from 5,695 ms on `main` to 894 ms. Cold went from 7,552 ms to 1,569 ms, and `flai stats --json` from 7,730 ms to 1,569 ms. Both builds give the same report. Half of what is left of a warm call is one `git log` for `claims.drift` per call. That is outside I-0155's cause, so it is recorded as I-0184 rather than fixed here. The tens-of-milliseconds aim of T-1993 is therefore met for the replay (71 ms on the generated history) but not for the whole `stats.get`.
