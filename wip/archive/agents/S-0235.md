## Decisions

- The planner's five tasks are kept as drafted, in three layers: T-1234 (flai stamp) and T-1235 (flaiover stamp) first, with no path in common; T-1236 after T-1234 and T-1237 after T-1235, because each builds what its layer-1 task reads; T-1238 (docs) last, after both, so that it describes what was built.
- flai's stamp is two linker variables in `buildinfo`, `ReleaseStatement` and `ReleaseSignature`. `flai version --json` shows them under `release`, with `state` (`signed`, `missing`, `invalid`), `reason`, and `development`. The statement's commit is GoReleaser's `{{ .ShortCommit }}`, the value `Commit` is given.
- flaiover's stamp is a file, `/app/release-stamp` by default, or the path `FLAIOVER_RELEASE_STAMP` names. It holds two lines: the statement and the base64 signature. The statement's commit is `FLAIOVER_COMMIT`.
- The flaiover workflow signs the stamp on top of S-0234's `release-key` job structure (MS-0060), because the key's secrets are in the `release` environment, which only tag runs may enter.
- S-0234 was accepted while T-1238 closed. The rebase conflicted in `release-flaiover.yml` and `design/tech/docker.md`. The stamp's signing is folded into S-0234's `release-key` job, which gains a checkout, cosign, the sign step, its outputs, and `contents: read`. S-0234's `image` job gains the check step and the two build arguments. Its Tags row in `docker.md` is kept, and the stamp stays in the Image row (MS-0060).
