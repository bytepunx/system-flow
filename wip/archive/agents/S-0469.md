# S-0469 Ready stories waited up to 3.5 days unheld with in-progress lanes free and review under its limit

## Context

I-0153's five E-0016 stories, S-0212 to S-0216, did not wait 75 h unheld in ready. The operator sent all five back to backlog on 2026-10-04 at 00:41Z and made them ready again on 2026-10-07 at 03:29Z. `queue_time_seconds` is `started - committed`, with `committed` the first move to ready, so it counted the 269,000 s in backlog. Their time in ready was held almost whole: S-0216 was 35,789 s in ready and held 35,693 s; S-0212 26,704 s and 26,665 s. The analysis read queue time minus `held_seconds` as unheld ready wait. flai serve's start rules were not at fault.

## Current state

Done and in review. ADR-0190 makes queue time the time in `ready` before `started`; `flai/internal/metrics/metrics.go` derives it so, with `TestQueueTimeLeavesOutABackToBacklog` replaying S-0216's moves (it fails against `started - committed`). I-0153 is closed. The close-out passed at 90cae2651 with integration and smoke left to the merge queue. Criteria 1 and 2 ticked.

## Decisions

- Remedy: queue time becomes the seconds an item spent in `ready` before `started`, the sum of its `ready` intervals, by an ADR, since `metrics.md` is the contract with the dashboard. For an item never sent back out of ready it is unchanged; `committed` stays the first move to ready. Rejected: moving `committed` to the last move to ready, which would leave the first ready stretch's holds outside queue time and make `held_seconds` exceed it.
- Plan: T-2064 (ADR and `design/system/metrics.md`) first; T-2065 (`flai/internal/metrics`) after it, so the code follows the recorded definition. No path in common, but the order is the reason. Both are small, so I work them myself rather than through task sub-agents.
