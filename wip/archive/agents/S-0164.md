# S-0164 Reviews need real diffs, not just line counts

## Context

S-0164 makes the review page's diff readable: a toggle control on each file's summary line, and a panel with the sign of each added and removed line in a margin, a dim background behind each line, and a brighter outline round each run of consecutive lines. Worktree `.flai-cache/worktrees/S-0164`, branch `story/S-0164`.

What is there already (S-0041): `flai stream diff` and the method `stream.diff` carry every file's hunks (`patch`), cut at 64 KB a file and 768 KB in all, and `flaiover/src/lib/components/DiffView.svelte` shows them when a file's summary line is clicked. Nothing on the line says it can be clicked. An open file shows the patch as text, the sign as the first character of each line, each line coloured on its own.

## Decisions

- No change to flai. The story claims `flai/cmd`, but `stream.diff` has carried each file's hunks since S-0041, and the panel needs nothing more. The work is in `flaiover/src`.
- The whole summary line stays the button, as the epic says ("by clicking the summary line for each file"), and the toggle control is the mark on it that shows the state.
- The text of an added or removed line keeps the theme's green or red, as S-0041 drew it. The story asks for the margin, the background, and the outline, and says nothing of the text.
- The outline and background are the theme's `good`, `danger`, and their soft steps, not new colours. They have a light and a dark value each, and `theme.test.ts` already checks their contrast.
- The sign in the margin is not selected with the text, so lines copied from a panel are the file's lines.
- A hunk header and a "no newline" note are lines of the panel with an empty margin. Line numbers are not drawn: the story does not ask for them.
- I-0027 is bumped on the story branch, as S-0163 did, so that nothing outside `wip/` is left uncommitted on main.
- Each task is committed on its own, as the operator's start instruction says. The git convention says to commit when the story lands. The operator's word takes precedence.
