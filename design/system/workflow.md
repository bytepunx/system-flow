# Workflow and board policies

This is the Lean part. The board is `wip/kanban`, the columns are the states in [work-hierarchy.md](work-hierarchy.md), and the policies below are what keeps flow visible and measurable. `wip/kanban/board.md` in each project records that project's WIP limits and any local policy; the defaults are here.

## Columns and WIP limits

| Column | Default WIP limit | Applies to |
|--------|-------------------|------------|
| backlog | none | all |
| ready | 5 | stories |
| in-progress | 2 | stories |
| review | 3 | stories |
| done | none; a story reaches done only by acceptance, which archives it | all |

Limits apply to stories. Tasks inherit their story's column budget. Epics are not limited; there should rarely be more than a handful open. `flai check` warns when a limit is exceeded. It does not block, because an agent finishing a story is more valuable than a hard stop, but the warning is recorded in the narrative. `flai check --strict` fails on ready or in-progress over its limit, but passes over review over its limit: only an acceptance clears it, the operator's or the orchestrator's ([What waits on an acceptance](#what-waits-on-an-acceptance-s-0296)), so it must not stop a story's close-out. It counts in the result's `advisory` and the summary line says so. A full review holds the pull instead (S-0243, [ADR-0073](../adrs/0073-a-full-review-holds-the-pull-and-flai-check-strict-passes-over-review-over-its.md)). `--strict` passes over `epic.lags-stories` for the same reason: an epic behind its stories is the operator's to move, not a story's agent's (S-0200, [An epic follows its stories](#an-epic-follows-its-stories)). The summary line counts both: `(N that --strict passes over: only the operator clears them, by accepting or by moving an epic)`.

## Pull policy

Work is pulled, not pushed. An agent starting a session:

1. Reads `wip/agents/index.md` to find streams with open narratives it was working on.
2. If none, reads `wip/kanban/board.md` and pulls the highest ordered `ready` story into `in-progress`, respecting the in-progress limit. While review is at or over its limit, it pulls nothing and waits for acceptance (S-0243, [ADR-0073](../adrs/0073-a-full-review-holds-the-pull-and-flai-check-strict-passes-over-review-over-its.md)).
3. Opens or resumes the narrative for that story in `wip/agents/<story-id>.md`.
4. If the story has no tasks, reads its goal, acceptance criteria, and notes and writes them, each with `## Work` and `## Done when`. If the planner drafted them (S-0255), reviews them against the story first and changes, adds, or cancels what it would plan differently. If the story does not say enough to do that, blocks it with the reason, opens a thread on it saying what is missing, and pulls the next story.
5. Works tasks in order, transitioning each task as it goes.

## Definition of ready (story)

- `## Goal` and `## Acceptance criteria` are filled.
- Parent epic is not `cancelled`.
- The story is not a `draft` (S-0199).

A draft is a story an agent wrote, by the planner or from an issue, that the operator has not yet read ([ADR-0074](../adrs/0074-work-items-carry-planning-data-a-story-s-draft-flag-an-epic-s-or-story-s-cost.md)). `flai move` refuses a draft to ready with "finalize it first". The operator finalizes it: `flai move <story> ready --yes` finalizes it as it moves, `flai edit <story> --no-draft` finalizes it in place, and the dashboard's `item.move` with `finalize` runs the move with `--yes`. No agent finalizes but the orchestrator: MCP's `item_move` refuses a draft to ready, and `item_edit` refuses `draft: false`, so an agent says in a thread or its narrative that the story is ready to be finalized. The orchestrator finalizes a draft that `flai promote --drafts` finds complete, with `item_edit` giving `draft: false` alone or `flai edit --no-draft`, only while the operator gives it `finalize_drafts`, and then moves it to ready as a candidate like any other (S-0219, [strategic-agents.md](strategic-agents.md#what-it-does-with-each-permission-s-0219)). `flai check` warns (`story.draft`) on a draft in ready or later.

Tasks are not part of ready. The agent that pulls the story writes them once it is `in-progress`, or reviews those the planner drafted in the backlog when the operator asked it to plan the story ([strategic-agents.md](strategic-agents.md)), and a story cannot enter `review` without at least one ([ADR-0021](../adrs/0021-story-ready-without-tasks.md)). `flai move` and `flai check` enforce both halves: nothing about tasks is checked on the way into `ready` or `in-progress`, and `story.tasks` is raised for `review` and `done` stories.

## Definition of done (story)

- All acceptance criteria ticked. The story's agent ticks each once it has verified it, through flai by its number (`flai criteria tick`, the MCP tool `criteria_tick`, or the dashboard's `POST /api/items/[id]/criteria`), never by editing the item's file; a task sub-agent names the criteria its task meets, and the story's agent ticks them after its review ([ADR-0089](../adrs/0089-acceptance-criteria-are-ticked-through-flai-by-number-by-the-story-s-agent-once.md)). `flai move` refuses done while one is unticked.
- All tasks `done` or `cancelled`.
- Any decision made during the story is captured: an ADR if it is architectural, an edit to `design/system` or `design/tech` if it changes the living design, a note in the story otherwise.
- User-facing behaviour changes have a matching edit under `docs/`.
- Narrative in `wip/agents/<story-id>.md` has a closing entry.

## Transitions and who makes them

| Transition | Made by | Required action |
|------------|---------|-----------------|
| backlog to ready | Human or agent during refinement; a draft story, the operator only, or the orchestrator once it has finalized it. The orchestrator moves only a candidate of `flai promote --candidates`, while ready is under its limit and it has `promote_to_ready` (S-0219) | Definition of ready met; a draft is finalized first or by the move (`--yes`) |
| ready to in-progress | Agent pulling work | Narrative opened; tasks written next if the story has none |
| in-progress to review | Agent | At least one task exists, no open question in the narrative, nothing uncommitted in the story's worktree (S-0140: `flai move` and `item_move` refuse it, naming the paths), acceptance criteria self-checked and ticked through flai (`flai move` and `item_move` warn, counting them, when one is unticked, and do not refuse: one that cannot be verified stays unticked with the reason in the story's notes, S-0282), narrative summary current |
| review to done | Human, or agent if the story is tagged `auto-accept`; the orchestrator, with `flai accept --by orchestrator`, while `orchestration.permissions.accept_reviews` is on and the verifier passed at the branch head, every criterion is ticked, the diff stays within the touches, no thread is open, and its evidence covers every criterion ([ADR-0093](../adrs/0093-with-accept-reviews-on-the-orchestrator-accepts-a-story-in-review-through-flai.md), [strategic-agents.md](strategic-agents.md#accepting-a-story-s-0221)); for an epic, the acceptance of its last open story | Definition of done met. For a story this transition is acceptance, however it is made: `flai accept`, `flai move <story> done`, a card dropped on done, or the item page button all run the same flow (S-0046). `flai accept` does the acceptance: rebase and merge the story branch, move to done, archive, resolve every thread still open or answered on what it archived, with the entry `Resolved: <id> was accepted`, and commit, the threads included (S-0277, [ADR-0109](../adrs/0109-archiving-an-item-resolves-the-threads-still-open-on-it.md)). An open thread does not stop the operator's acceptance, which settles it; it stops the orchestrator's. Its preview lists anything uncommitted in the story's worktree (`worktree_uncommitted`) and blocks on it before anything is merged; `flai serve agent commit <story>`, or **Have an agent commit them** in the dashboard's confirmation, starts the story's agent to commit it (S-0140). Nothing is tagged or pushed at acceptance (S-0087, ADR-0032): publishing what has accumulated is `flai release --pending`, a deliberate step of its own, run by hand or from the board's Publish action, see `design/conventions/git.md`. Publishing is the one way accepted work reaches the remote: `git fetch`, then `flai release --pending` or Publish, which tags and pushes the branch and the tags, never forcing, and refuses a clone behind its remote's tags or branch. The operator publishes, or an agent the operator asks; no agent pushes accepted work on its own ([ADR-0067](../adrs/0067-accepted-work-reaches-the-remote-only-when-it-is-published-and-agents-publish.md)) |
| review to in-progress | Human | Reason appended to story notes |
| an epic's backlog to ready, ready to in-progress, in-progress to review, and back | Its stories' moves; the operator by hand | The epic follows its stories ([below](#an-epic-follows-its-stories)). A hand move is still allowed |
| ready to backlog, in-progress to ready | Human | A step back one column, from the board's lane menu or `flai move` ([ADR-0055](../adrs/0055-a-story-moves-back-one-column-from-ready-in-progress-review-or-cancelled-and.md)). A reason is optional and appended to the notes when given. A story back in ready goes last in the ready order; one back in backlog leaves the order |
| cancelled to backlog | Human | Reopens a cancelled item; refused while its parent is cancelled, which is moved back first. What its cancellation cancelled under it stays cancelled. The item is not completed again until it closes ([ADR-0055](../adrs/0055-a-story-moves-back-one-column-from-ready-in-progress-review-or-cancelled-and.md)) |
| done to anything | Nobody | Done is final: acceptance merged and archived the work |
| backlog, ready, or in-progress to cancelled | Human | Reason appended to the item's notes. Everything open under the item is cancelled with it ([ADR-0028](../adrs/0028-cancelling-an-item-cancels-everything-open-under-it.md)) |
| review to cancelled | Only a parent's cancellation | An item in review is accepted or sent back; it is cancelled only with its epic or story. An epic in review has no parent: it is moved back to in-progress first, or its stories are accepted |

`flai move <id> <state>` performs a transition, appends it to `transitions`, updates `status` and `updated`, and validates the rule for that transition. A `--reason` is recorded under the item's `## Notes`. Tasks may skip `review` and go from `in-progress` to `done` directly. A move to `cancelled` takes everything open under the item with it, in one operation: an epic's stories that are not done or cancelled and their open tasks, a story's open tasks. Each gets its own `cancelled` transition with the same actor and time and a note naming the item whose cancellation caused it; done, cancelled, and archived items are left alone; every move is validated before any file is written. A cancelled story moves its epic forward as [below](#an-epic-follows-its-stories); the stories an epic's cancellation takes with it do not. `flai move` lists the items first, asks on a terminal unless `--yes`, and changes nothing under `--dry-run`; the board shows the same list in its confirmation. Branches, worktrees, and narratives are never removed by a cancellation; the command names what it left. `flai check` reports an open item under a cancelled parent as `item.parent-cancelled`. A story moved from `review` to `done` is accepted rather than merely transitioned; a story found `done` but unarchived was never accepted, `flai check` flags it as `story.unaccepted`, and `flai accept` completes it. The board's `order` list is the pull order: ready stories first, then backlog stories in the order they should be refined.

### An epic follows its stories

Since S-0200 an epic's state is a summary of its stories' ([ADR-0076](../adrs/0076-an-epic-follows-its-stories-to-ready-and-in-progress-with-the-first-to-review.md)). Its stories, cancelled ones left out and archived ones counted, put it in a state: done when all are done, review when all are in review or done, in-progress when any has started (in-progress, review, or done), ready when any is ready, else backlog. An epic with no story that counts stays where it is.

When a story moves, its epic moves toward that state in the same write, but only in the direction the story moved:

- Forward, when the story moved forward or was cancelled. The epic goes to ready with its first ready story, to in-progress with its first started one, and to review with its last open one. It never goes to done this way: a cancelled last story, with the others done, takes it to review at most, for the operator to accept.
- Back, when the story moved back or came back from cancelled, and only as far as no other story holds it.

The epic walks the allowed transitions one at a time, each with the story's actor and time and a note in the epic's Notes naming the story, such as `follows S-0001, which moved to ready`, as a cancellation's cascade does ([ADR-0028](../adrs/0028-cancelling-an-item-cancels-everything-open-under-it.md)). Every step is validated with the story's move before any file is written: a step the epic cannot take refuses the move, and nothing changes. An epic that is done, cancelled, or archived does not follow. No command creates a story in ready, so a story entering ready is the only way an epic is taken there.

Every way of moving a story does this: `flai move`, MCP's `item_move`, the dashboard's move, which runs `flai move`, and a cancellation. `flai move` prints the epic's move under the item's line, indented: `E-0001 → in-progress, following S-0001`, and `--json` returns it as `followed` (`id`, `type`, `title`, `from`, `to`, `story`); a cancellation's list, its `--json`, and its dry run say it too. `item_move`'s result carries `followed`. The MCP `inbox` and `wait_for_events` describe the epic's change as `E-0001 <title> moved to in-progress by <who>, following S-0001`, and the change carries `follows`. That is read from the transitions' shared time and actor, as a cascade's cause is; the front matter records nothing new, so a hand move of the epic in the same second by the same actor reads as following.

Acceptance (`flai accept`, `flai move <story> done`, the dashboard) of an epic's last open story walks the epic to done with it and archives the epic, with its cancelled stories, in the one acceptance commit, subject `chore: [S-nnnn] accept and archive, with E-nnnn`. The dry run says `would also move E-0001 <title> from review to done, following S-0001, and archive it`, and a walk or archive that would fail is a blocker before anything is merged. `--json` carries `epic` (`id`, `type`, `title`, `from`, `to`, `story`), and the progress log has an `epic` step between done and archived. An epic accepted this way, or by hand, contributes no release bump of its own: its stories carry theirs ([ADR-0078](../adrs/0078-an-accepted-epic-contributes-no-release-bump-of-its-own-its-stories-carry-theirs.md), `design/conventions/git.md`).

A story given another epic with `flai edit --parent`, or from the dashboard, moves the epic it joined as though it had entered from backlog, and the one it left as though it had been cancelled out of it, so forward only and short of done. `flai edit` prints `E-0004 → in-progress, following S-0085`, indented, for each, and `--json` lists them in `followed`.

The operator can still move an epic by hand with `flai move`. A forward story move never pulls an epic back, so an epic moved ahead of its stories stays there until a story moves back. Epics from before S-0200, or moved back by hand, lag their stories until a story moves: `flai check` warns about each open epic that its stories put further on than its status (`epic.lags-stories`, advisory), naming the moves that catch it up, or `flai accept E-nnnn` when its stories are all done. `--strict` passes over it ([Columns and WIP limits](#columns-and-wip-limits)).

### Natures that do not release ([ADR-0025](../adrs/0025-research-is-accepted-without-a-release.md), [ADR-0066](../adrs/0066-an-experiment-story-is-accepted-like-any-other-and-records-its-results-in-a.md))

Acceptance of a `research` or `experiment` story is the same act as any other (merge, done, archive, commit) since S-0087: no acceptance tags or pushes any more. What is still true: publishing (`flai release --pending`, [ADR-0032](../adrs/0032-accepting-a-story-merges-it-publishing-is-a-deliberate-batched-step-over.md)) never gives a component a bump on a research story's or an experiment's account, whatever it touched; the plan for one (`flai release <id>`) says it releases nothing and names each component whose files it touched as landing on main unreleased, and the next story that delivers to the component releases them.

An `experiment` story records its results in a document of its own, `design/experiments/<S-nnnn>-<slug>.md`, named as the story's file is, with the hypothesis, the success measure, what was done, the results, and a recommendation to adopt, adapt, or drop ([repository-layout.md](repository-layout.md#design)). Acceptance, from `flai accept`, `flai move <story> done`, or the board, refuses an experiment whose branch has no such document before anything is merged, and names the document it expects; without a branch it looks in the main checkout. The document is found by the story's ID, so any `<S-nnnn>-*.md` in the folder counts. `flai check` validates it (`experiments.*`).

### The pull order (S-0057)

Only the `ready` and `backlog` columns have an order, and only stories are in it; the other columns are read by what happened to the items in them, and tasks follow their story. A column reads as the stories `order` names, in its order, then the stories it does not name, by ID. So a backlog nobody has prioritised still has one reading, and placing a story says something only about the stories placed above it.

`flai order <story> --before <other> | --after <other> | --top | --bottom` places a story within its column. It refuses an epic or a task, a story in any other state, a reference in another column (changing the column is `flai move`), and a story relative to itself, each with the reason. On every write the list is normalised: ready stories, then backlog stories, and names that are no longer ready or backlog stories are dropped. Backlog stories that were never placed and still come last by ID stay unnamed, so one placement does not make `board.md` name the whole backlog. Ready stories are always named.

`flai move` keeps the list true as stories change column: a story moved to `ready` is named after the ready stories already named and before any backlog story, wherever the list had it; a story moved anywhere else is removed, so one sent back to `backlog` returns to the unplaced ones. `flai board`, the MCP `board` and `inbox` tools, and the dashboard's board all lay out `backlog` and `ready` in this sequence, and the dashboard's drag within a column calls `flai order` (ADR-0016). An agent's MCP `inbox` reports a reordering of the stories it had already seen as a change.

`flai order` records who placed a story and when, in a `placed` map in `board.md`'s front matter after `order`, keyed by story, each entry `by` and `at` (S-0219, [ADR-0088](../adrs/0088-board-md-records-who-placed-a-story-by-hand-and-when-and-a-policy-s-order-keeps.md)). Each placement replaces the story's entry. `by` is `--placed-by`, else `FLAI_AGENT`, else the config author, so the dashboard's drag is recorded as `flaiover`; under `FLAI_ROLE=orchestrate` it is always `orchestrator`. `flai move` drops a story's entry when it takes the story out of its column. `flai order --by <policy> --apply` records no placement: it is a policy's order, not a hand's.

A policy's order keeps a hand placement of the last day. `flai order --by <policy>` keeps each ready story that anyone but the orchestrator placed within `--keep-placed`, a day unless given, at its position, and orders the rest around it; the listing marks it `(kept: placed by <by> at <at>)`, and `--keep-placed 0` keeps none. The orchestrator orders the ready column with `--apply` at the default window, and the MCP tool `order_by_policy` reads the same order, so a story the operator drags holds against the orchestrator for a day ([strategic-agents.md](strategic-agents.md#what-it-does-with-each-permission-s-0219)).

Ready means work starts (S-0079). An agent that is running and idle holds `wait_for_work` (S-0097), which names the story to pull as soon as one is ready, not held (below), the in-progress limit leaves room, and review is under its limit; `inbox` lists ready stories to one that ends its turns. When none is, and the operator has enabled it on the host, `flai serve` starts each ready story's agent in this order: once per story entering ready, and again when its agent is changed while it is in ready (S-0116), only while the in-progress limit leaves room and review is under its limit, and not while the story is held, whoever is attending ([ADR-0043](../adrs/0043-flai-serve-starts-a-ready-story-s-agent-whenever-the-in-progress-limit-has-room.md), [ADR-0046](../adrs/0046-a-ready-story-whose-claim-overlaps-an-open-story-s-is-held-yellow-and-with-its.md)). A full review holds the pull (S-0243, [ADR-0073](../adrs/0073-a-full-review-holds-the-pull-and-flai-check-strict-passes-over-review-over-its.md)): flai serve starts no agent for a ready story and says why (`review is full (5 of 5): accept or send back a story`), `inbox` and `wait_for_work` report `can_pull` false with the reason in `pull_hold`, and `wait_for_work` waits with `waiting_for: review`, where a full in-progress limit gives `room`. Stories in progress, and agents already started, go on. A story in progress, not blocked, whose agent ended failed, neither asking nor stopped by the operator, gets a new one from flai serve at its next look, as many times as the host's `agent.auto_restarts` allows (2 when unset, 0 for none) since the last run flai serve did not start on its own; at the limit flai serve opens one thread on the story for the operator instead (S-0294, [ADR-0108](../adrs/0108-flai-serve-restarts-a-story-s-agent-that-ended-with-its-story-in-progress-up-to.md)). A story in ready or in progress whose agent dropped or failed gets a new one on the operator's word: `flai serve agent restart`, or Retry on its page. For a story in ready while the in-progress limit is full, review is full, or the story is held, that queues the agent, and flai serve starts it once there is room and it is clear (S-0118, [ADR-0044](../adrs/0044-a-retry-of-a-failed-agent-on-a-ready-story-with-the-in-progress-limit-full-is.md)). A story in ready gets its agent at once on the operator's word, whatever the launcher's own rules say, past the limit, a full review, and a hold with a warning: `flai serve agent start`, or Start agent on its page (S-0115). See the operators' guide for what enabling it means.

### What waits on an acceptance (S-0296)

A story in review holds no ready story by overlap ([ADR-0096](../adrs/0096-a-story-in-review-holds-nothing-an-overlap-inside-the-manifest-s-shared-paths.md)). Two waits on its acceptance remain:

- A ready story that names it in `after:` is held until it is done ([After](#branches-and-collisions-adr-0019)).
- While review is at its limit, every ready story waits: flai serve starts no agent and `wait_for_work` offers none ([above](#the-pull-order-s-0057)). Sending a story back clears this wait as well as accepting it.

While the operator alone accepts, both waits last as long as the operator is away, and the board stands idle ([I-0088](../issues/I-0088-a-story-waits-in-review-for-the-operator-while-nothing-else-can-start-so-the-board-stands-idle-until-a-person-accepts.md)). With the `orchestrate` host action on and `orchestration.permissions.accept_reviews` on, the orchestrator accepts a story in review under [ADR-0093](../adrs/0093-with-accept-reviews-on-the-orchestrator-accepts-a-story-in-review-through-flai.md)'s conditions ([Accepting a story](strategic-agents.md#accepting-a-story-s-0221)), and the stories waiting on it start without a person. A wait remains only for a story the orchestrator cannot vouch for, which it leaves in review with a thread saying what is missing, and for one the operator keeps to review, by leaving a thread open on it.

## Blocking

When an agent cannot proceed it appends an open `blocked` interval with a reason, writes the question in the narrative under `## Open questions`, and moves on to another task or story if one is available. When the block clears, the interval is closed. The item never leaves its column because of a block.

## Cadence

There are no sprints. Flow is continuous. The dashboard's charts replace the status meeting: cycle time scatter shows whether stories are getting slower, burn-up shows scope versus completion per epic, and the state-time breakdown shows where time is spent. Reviewing those charts weekly and adjusting WIP limits or definitions is the process improvement loop.

## Branches and collisions (ADR-0019)

Each story is worked on `story/S-nnnn` in a worktree under `.flai-cache/worktrees/`; `wip/` is written in the main checkout so the board is live. Agents work a story task by task ([ADR-0069](../adrs/0069-agents-commit-each-task-on-the-story-branch-after-flai-stream-sync-and-never.md)): when a task is done they commit its changes, with its docs and work item updates, on `story/S-nnnn`; run `flai stream sync`, resolving each conflict it lists, `git add` and `git rebase --continue`; then run the tests for what the task changed and commit any fix they need. Before moving the story to review they commit what is outstanding, sync again, and close out with `scripts/close-out.sh`, which refuses a branch that does not contain the main branch. The close-out ends every run with one line giving the story, the outcome, and the step it stopped at. The verifier the story's agent hands the close-out to runs it once, in one command without a pipe or a file, and reads that line; the agent tells it any step it already knows will stop, so that it reports that stop and checks the steps after it rather than running the close-out again (S-0266). `flai stream sync` is how agents rebase a story branch; they never start a `git rebase` or `git merge` by hand. It never stashes: it refuses, touching nothing, a worktree with uncommitted changes, naming each path, or with a rebase already in progress; when the rebase stops on conflicts it lists each path with how to continue and how to abort (`git rebase --abort` puts the branch back as it was before the sync), and exits non-zero. `flai accept` rebases and merges. Between the two it refuses, merging nothing, a branch any of whose added or changed files carries a merge conflict marker, the lines `flai check` reports as `markdown.conflict-marker`, naming each path and line, so that a conflict added to git unresolved during a sync never reaches the main branch (S-0253, I-0066). Stories and tasks may declare `touches`; `flai check` warns when two in-progress items overlap outside the shared paths (below) and the dashboard badges the documents. The designer edits on `main` through the dashboard, so their intent reaches the next sync.

[ADR-0046](../adrs/0046-a-ready-story-whose-claim-overlaps-an-open-story-s-is-held-yellow-and-with-its.md) (S-0124) turns `touches` into a claim that scheduling acts on. The survey behind it is [agent-coordination.md](agent-coordination.md). S-0128 built the hold, and S-0295 ([ADR-0096](../adrs/0096-a-story-in-review-holds-nothing-an-overlap-inside-the-manifest-s-shared-paths.md), I-0087) narrowed it in three ways: a story in review holds nothing, shared paths never hold, and tasks narrow a folder claim:

- **Claim.** A story's claim is its `touches` and its tasks', each entry read as a path first: an entry that is a sub-project's name or tag in `system-flow.yaml` is that sub-project's path, so `cli` and `flai` are both `flai`. A story touch that is a folder holding touches of the story's tasks, done or open but not cancelled, is replaced by those touches. A story touch that no task names inside stays whole. An open task's touch outside every story touch is added. A done task's files stay claimed, because the branch changed them. `flai stream sync` reports a file the branch changed outside the narrowed claim, so the agent widens the task's touches. The planner and the story's agent declare touches file by file, and keep a folder only where files no task can name yet may be added.
- **Open.** A story is open while it is in progress or in review, and its claim counts for `wip.overlap`, the trial merge at sync, and the notice at acceptance. Only a story in progress holds a ready story. A story in review holds nothing: its branch is finished and synced, and the notice at acceptance tells an overlapping story what changed. To flai serve, a story in ready whose agent it has started is in progress too, so one look never starts two stories that overlap.
- **Shared paths.** `claims.shared` in `system-flow.yaml` lists glob patterns of paths that many stories change in separate sections or new files, such as `docs/users/flai.md` and `design/adrs` ([project-manifest.md](project-manifest.md)). An overlapping pair of entries does not hold when the narrower of the two lies wholly inside a pattern. A folder lies inside only when all of it does: `docs/users` is not inside `docs/users/*.md`. Such an overlap is not a `wip.overlap` and not a grown claim either, but the trial merge at sync and the notice at acceptance still report it, so a real conflict there is still caught. `flai shared` lists, checks, adds, and removes the patterns, as do the host API, the dashboard's project settings, and the MCP tools `shared_paths` and `shared_paths_edit` ([flai-cli.md](flai-cli.md#commands)). Only the operator's own session may change them: `flai guard` refuses every session flai serve starts.
- **Held.** A ready story is held when its claim overlaps the claim of a story in progress by the `/`-prefix rule `flai check` uses, outside the shared paths (`overlap`), when its own claim or that of a story in progress is empty (`no-touches`), or when a story it names in `after:` is not done (`after`, S-0130).
- **After.** `after: [S-nnnn, …]` names stories that must be done first, whatever they touch ([work-hierarchy.md](work-hierarchy.md#identifiers)). The reason names each with its state and says what clears it: `held (after): waits for S-0129 (in progress); starts when S-0129 is done`. A cancelled story named keeps the hold, and the reason says to drop it from `after:` if the story no longer needs it; a story that does not exist keeps it too, and `flai check` reports it. A story held both ways is held `after`, and its reason goes on `; also held (overlap): …`. Named stories are looked up in the archive too, so an accepted story clears the hold. A story can wait only for a story named in its own `after:`, and `flai check` refuses a cycle, so the pull order does not deadlock.
- **Skip-ahead.** flai serve does not start a held story's agent, and `wait_for_work` does not offer it. Both take the next ready story in pull order that is not held, within the in-progress limit and while review is under its limit. The held story keeps its place and is taken first once it is clear.
- **The reason.** It names the path, the story in progress, and what clears it: `held (overlap): touches flai/cmd/serve, inside flai/cmd which S-0128 (in progress) touches; starts when S-0128 moves to review, is cancelled, or is sent back`. Every story that holds it is named. `flai board` prints it under the card (`HELD`), and `flai board --json`, `board.get`, and the MCP `board`, `inbox`, and `wait_for_work` answers carry it as `held: {code, reason}` on the ready card. `wait_for_work` times out with `waiting_for: held` when every ready story with room is held. `agent.status` reports a held story as `waiting` with the reason and `hold`, whether or not it has had an agent.
- **The operator's word.** `flai move <story> in-progress` and `item_move` warn and move it. `flai serve agent start` warns and starts it, and tells the agent in its prompt, and the journal, that the operator started it and the hold it went past, so the agent does not take it for flai serve's own start or narrow its touches only to clear the hold (S-0182, I-0050). `flai serve agent restart` of a held story in ready queues it.
- **An answered agent.** flai serve starts an agent that ended asking again once its question is answered, in its session (a recommendation awaiting the operator's confirmation is no answer: the confirmation, or another answer, is, S-0220), at once while its story is in progress or in review: an open story is not held. A story sent back to ready meanwhile waits for its hold, the limit, and a full review like any other, and one in backlog is not started (S-0182). Since S-0272 a story's agent ends on its question rather than waiting for the answer: it asks on a thread, goes on with what does not wait on the answer, and when nothing else is left writes its narrative's `## Current state` and `## Next steps` and ends ([work-management.md](../conventions/work-management.md)). flai serve records the run `asked` and starts it again in its session on the answer, which its first `inbox` holds. An agent that holds `wait_for_events` instead is told to end: the tool answers at once with `end: true` and `why`, after any events already behind the cursor, when flai serve started it for its story (`FLAI_STORY` set, no `FLAI_ROLE`), a thread on the story or one of its tasks awaits an answer to its own question, and no task of the story is in progress. That is serve's own rule for a run it starts again, so an agent told to end is one it restarts. An agent with a task in progress, one run by hand, and the planner, the orchestrator, and the analyzer still hold. A story's agent's call that times out with nothing to report is an empty wake, counted from its logs into the story's `usage.empty_wakes` and reported by `flai stats` under `waiting`, so that the saving is measured ([ADR-0105](../adrs/0105-a-story-s-empty-wakes-the-wait-for-events-calls-of-its-agents-that-timed-out.md)).

S-0129 built the dashboard's yellow card: a held story's card and page say why it waits and link the story it waits for ([flaiover-dashboard.md](flaiover-dashboard.md)). Still to come under E-0009, in this order: `after:`, and the trial merge and drift check at `flai stream sync`.

S-0132 built the notice at acceptance. When a story is accepted, flai lists the paths its merge brought into the main branch. Every other open story whose claim covers one of them, shared paths included, is told which paths those are. A story with an empty claim is told of every path, because it may change anything. The notice is appended to `.flai-cache/overlaps.jsonl`. The MCP `inbox` and `wait_for_events` report it once, as a change of kind `overlapped` on the open story, with `cause` the accepted story and `to` the paths. `flai accept` prints whom it told and returns the list as `overlaps` in `--json`. On such a notice the agent runs `flai stream sync` and its tests again before it goes on ([work-management.md](../conventions/work-management.md)). A story with no branch merged tells nobody. The notices are best effort: a notice that cannot be worked out is logged, and the acceptance stands.

S-0244 tells of a claim that grows after the pull (I-0059). A story's claim widens as its tasks are written, and two stories that passed the hold can end up claiming the same paths. After `flai task new`, `flai edit --touches`, `flai touches`, and the MCP `item_new` of a task and `item_edit` of `touches`, flai compares the paths the written story's claim gained with the claim of every other story in progress; only a story in progress is compared, and only the paths gained count, so an overlap already allowed is not told again. An overlap inside the shared paths is not told (S-0295). The command names each other story it reached and returns them as `overlaps`, and both stories get an `overlapped` change whose `cause` is the other story and `to` the paths. The agent coordinates with the other story's agent on a thread before it changes those paths, and narrows its touches if it can. The write always stands: `wip.overlap` stays a warning ([ADR-0019](../adrs/0019-story-branches-and-touches.md)).
